Stress & Communication Delay

Experimental research in simulated long-duration spaceflight

The Research Question

How does communication delay affect stress during simulated long-duration spaceflight, and do individual personality differences help explain variation in the stress response?

Experimental Design Stress Personality Spaceflight
100 Participants
87 Physiological Sample
45 min Mission Duration

Study Design

Participants completed simulated mission scenarios under different communication conditions. The repeated-measures design allowed responses to be compared within participants.

Measures

Subjective Measures

Self-reported stress and perceived communication quality were used to capture participants' experience during the missions.

Physiological Measures

Heart-rate data were collected using a Polar H10 chest strap and used to derive physiological stress indicators.

My Role

  • Conducted statistical analyses and evaluated model assumptions.

The analytical approach was refined after examining the relationships among the stress measures, rather than relying solely on the initially considered statistical model.

Key Findings

Communication quality changed significantly under communication delay, while the expected increase in stress was not observed.

The findings illustrate how changes in the communication environment can affect the participant experience without necessarily producing the same change in physiological or subjective stress.

Why It Matters

Human performance in long-duration missions depends on more than system capability. Communication conditions, individual differences, and the way people experience system constraints can all influence how humans interact with complex environments.

Publication
