Human-Participant Research Practices

Examining participant practices in HCI games research

The Research Question

How are human participants recruited, characterized, and studied across HCI games research, and how consistently are these practices reported?

Research Areas Examined

Context

Human participants are central to HCI and games research, but information about who participates and how studies are conducted is not always reported consistently.

This project examined reporting practices across published HCI games research to better understand how participants, recruitment procedures, and study methods are documented.

Study Design

Literature Search Study Selection Data Extraction Mapping & Analysis

The review examined full papers from four CHI PLAY conferences and games-related papers from four CHI conferences.

A total of 384 human-participant studies was selected and analyzed across participant characteristics, recruitment procedures, and study design and methods.

What I Examined

  • Participant characteristics, including participant type, gender, and age.
  • Recruitment procedures, including compensation and attrition.
  • Study characteristics, including study duration and research methods.
  • Consistency in how human-participant information was reported across published studies.

My Role

  • Conducted a systematic mapping review of human-participant research practices.
  • Examined participant and recruitment reporting across published HCI games research.
  • Organized and analyzed study characteristics and research methods.
  • Synthesized patterns and inconsistencies across the research literature.
  • Contributed to recommendations for improving reporting practices in human-participant research.

Research Methods & Analysis

Review Methods

  • Systematic literature search
  • Study selection and organization
  • Structured data extraction
  • Participant characteristic coding
  • Recruitment and study-method coding

Analysis

  • Descriptive analysis of participant characteristics
  • Analysis of recruitment and compensation practices
  • Analysis of study duration and research methods
  • Cross-study comparison of reporting practices

Key Findings

The review identified inconsistent reporting practices across human-participant studies in HCI games research.

Participant characteristics, recruitment procedures, and study methods were not consistently documented across the literature, making it difficult to compare studies and understand who participates in HCI games research.

Why It Matters

Good participant reporting is important for understanding who research findings represent and for making studies easier to interpret, evaluate, and replicate.

The findings support the need for clearer standards and more consistent reporting of human-participant research practices in HCI and games research.
