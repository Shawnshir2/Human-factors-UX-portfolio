AI-Supported Learning

Evaluating AI-supported tools for research methods education

The Research Question

Can AI-supported learning tools improve students' perceived competence and learning in experimental research design and statistical reasoning?

Human-AI Interaction Mixed Methods Experimental Research Education Research
200 Students
3 Course Sections
15–30 min AI Interaction

Context

Students interacted with AI-supported learning tools designed to support research methods instruction. The study examined both students' perceptions of their competence and their objective knowledge.

Study Design

Pretest
→
AI Interaction
→
Posttest
→
Reflection

Students completed pre- and post-intervention measures alongside qualitative reflections and interaction data.

My Role

  • Designed and supported the research study.
  • Developed AI-supported learning activities.
  • Collaborated on course and workshop implementation.
  • Collected and analyzed quantitative and qualitative data.
  • Interpreted findings and translated them into instructional implications.

Measures & Analysis

Measures

  • Perceived competence
  • Research methods knowledge
  • Student experience and reflections
  • AI interaction transcripts

Analysis

  • Paired-samples t-tests
  • Regression analysis
  • Qualitative review of student responses and reflections

Key Findings

Perceived competence improved across all three course sections, while objective knowledge gains were not consistent across sections.

The results highlighted an important distinction between how capable students felt after interacting with the learning tools and what they demonstrated on knowledge measures.

Why It Matters

The study illustrates how AI-supported learning can be evaluated using both subjective and objective measures. Rather than treating engagement or perceived competence as evidence of learning on their own, the research considered multiple forms of evidence to evaluate the experience.
