Human Factors & Automation

Modeling trust in human-autonomy teams

The Research Problem

Human interaction with autonomous systems depends partly on how much users trust the system. Both insufficient trust and excessive trust can create challenges for effective human- autonomy teaming.

Human Factors Trust Human-Autonomy Teaming System Design

Research Question

How can psychological trust constructs be translated into measurable human-factors requirements for human-autonomy systems?

Human Factors → Engineering

A central goal of the work was connecting psychological research on trust with practical considerations for system evaluation and design.

My Contribution

  • Examined trust as a human-factors construct in human-autonomy teaming.
  • Connected psychological trust concepts to system-level evaluation.
  • Considered how trust could be operationalized through measurable constructs.
  • Helped translate the research problem into a framework relevant to engineering and system design.

Research-to-Design Translation

Psychological Constructs

Trust, reliance, expectations, and perceptions of system behavior.

Human Factors Measures

Observable and measurable indicators that can be used to evaluate human-system interaction.

System Evaluation

Testing whether system behavior supports appropriate levels of human reliance.

Design Implications

Using evidence about human behavior to inform the development and evaluation of autonomous systems.

Why It Matters

The value of human factors research is not only understanding people, but translating that understanding into requirements, measures, and decisions that improve system design.

This work demonstrates an approach to connecting behavioral science with engineering and human-autonomy system development.
