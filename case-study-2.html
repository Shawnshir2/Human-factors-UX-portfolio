Trust & Human-Autonomy Interaction

Modeling trust in human-autonomy teams

The Research Problem

Autonomous systems need to earn an appropriate level of user trust. Too much trust can lead to misuse, while too little can lead to disuse. This project examined which characteristics of an autonomous system most influence human trust and how those factors could inform the design and evaluation of human-autonomy interactions.

Human Factors UX Research Human-Autonomy Interaction Mixed Methods

Research Question

What factors most influence people's trust in autonomous systems, and how can those factors inform system design and evaluation?

Research Approach

Literature Review → Workshop Design → Participant Input → Data Collection → Analysis → Design Implications

Trust factors were identified through a review of existing human-autonomy trust research and selected for further investigation based on their relevance to the research context. A structured workshop was then used to collect participant perspectives and prioritize the factors that influenced trust.

Participatory Workshop

As part of the 2023 Winter Experimentation Campaign at the Defence Research and Development Canada Toronto Research Center, a feasibility workshop was conducted with 32 participants.

Participants explored the importance of different factors that could affect trust in an autonomous system. Twelve trust factors were presented across dispositional and situational categories.

Dispositional Factors

Age, personality, exposure to risk, mental demands, and experience with similar robots.

Situational Factors

Experience with the current robot, route effectiveness, timeliness, concealment, ease of use, transparency and feedback, and anthropomorphic characteristics.

Data Collection & Analysis

Workshop discussions provided qualitative insight into how participants understood trust, system performance, communication, and user expectations. Pairwise comparisons were then used to quantify the relative importance of the trust factors identified in the workshop.

What Participants Told Us

Participants consistently emphasized predictable performance, clear communication, meaningful system value, and understandable system behavior as important contributors to trust.

Consistent Performance

Participants associated trust with reliable, consistent, certain, and predictable system performance.

Clear Communication

System updates helped users avoid surprises, understand changes, and adapt their plans.

Meaningful Value

An autonomous system should provide useful capability rather than increase the user's work or effort.

Understandable Behavior

Participants wanted to understand what the system could do, its boundaries, and how it was behaving.

Trust Repair

When failures occurred, participants emphasized explanations followed by visible changes in behavior.

Expectation Management

Communication about delays or changes helped users adjust expectations and respond to changing situations.

Why It Matters

Trust is not simply a psychological characteristic of the user. It is shaped by how a system performs, communicates, responds to problems, and supports the user's goals.

This research demonstrated how behavioral research can be used to identify what matters to users, quantify the relative importance of those factors, and translate those findings into considerations for the design and evaluation of autonomous systems.
